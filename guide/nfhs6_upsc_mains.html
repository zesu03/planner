The survey at a glance

What NFHS-6 is

The sixth round of India's largest household health survey, conducted in 2023–24 by the Ministry of Health & Family Welfare with the International Institute for Population Sciences (IIPS), Mumbai as nodal agency. It covered 6,79,238 households, 7,16,397 women and 1,00,977 men across 715 districts in every State and UT except Manipur.

Design and method

Scope
Multi-stage stratified random sample producing estimates down to the district, with urban/rural strata. Fieldwork ran in two phases (May 2023 – Dec 2024) through 27 field agencies, using computer-assisted personal interviewing and a clinical–anthropometric–biochemical (CAB) component. For urban sampling, Urban Frame Survey blocks replaced the older Census Enumeration Blocks — a notable design change given that the Census itself is overdue.

First of its kind

13 new
The first round funded entirely by the Government of India, and the first since the pandemic. Thirteen indicators were added: direct benefit transfers, self-help group membership, digital and financial inclusion, and expanded clinical testing — a visible shift toward tracking welfare delivery and lifestyle disease rather than only survival.

The headline controversy

Critique
The fact sheet shrank from 131 indicators to 101: 43 dropped, 13 added. Gone are anaemia, infant and under-five mortality, sex ratio at birth, sanitation and clean cooking fuel. This is the single most examinable feature of NFHS-6 because it is not a health question — it is a question about statistical governance.

How to use this

Method
Read Why NFHS Matters first — most answers lose marks by treating NFHS as a data dump rather than as an institution. Then lock the pairs in Key Data, take a position using the Mains Toolkit, and write against the Question Bank. Precision Notes cover the figures most often misquoted.
The argument in one paragraph

NFHS-6 records a decade of successful access expansion — institutional delivery, immunisation, financial protection, women's digital and banking inclusion — while exposing three unfinished transitions: undernutrition that has plateaued even as stunting falls, a non-communicable disease burden rising faster than the health system's ability to absorb it, and an over-medicalisation of childbirth concentrated in the private sector. Layered over all of it is a fourth problem that is administrative rather than medical: the survey that measures these things has stopped measuring several of them.

Institutional significance

Why NFHS carries more weight than any other health survey

In a Mains answer, the value of citing NFHS comes from knowing what depends on it. India runs several statistical systems; only one of them produces district-level, household-linked, equity-disaggregated health data. When that system changes what it measures, a chain of indices, schemes and targets changes with it.

1. It is India's only district-level health database

The Sample Registration System gives reliable vital rates but essentially at State level. The NSS gives consumption and morbidity, not clinical outcomes. The Census gives population counts, not health. NFHS-4 first produced district estimates for 640 districts; NFHS-6 covers 715. This granularity is what makes the Aspirational Districts and Aspirational Blocks Programmes measurable, since their health and nutrition indicators are drawn largely from NFHS.

Use in: governance, decentralised planning, evidence-based policy

2. It is the sole data source for the National Multidimensional Poverty Index

NITI Aayog's National MPI is computed from NFHS household unit-level microdata across twelve SDG-aligned indicators in health, education and standard of living — including nutrition, child and adolescent mortality, maternal health, cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, electricity, housing, assets and bank accounts. The MPI headcount fell from 24.85% (NFHS-4) to 14.96% (NFHS-5), the basis for the claim that roughly 13.5 crore people exited multidimensional poverty in five years. No NFHS round means no MPI update; a changed NFHS means a discontinuous MPI series.

Use in: poverty measurement, GS-3 inclusive growth, GS-2 welfare schemes

3. It anchors SDG and National Health Policy monitoring

NITI Aayog's SDG India Index draws NFHS data for Goal 2 (stunting, wasting, underweight, anaemia), Goal 3 (maternal and child health, immunisation, insurance), Goal 5 (gender, spousal violence, decision-making), Goal 6 (sanitation) and Goal 7 (clean cooking fuel). National Health Policy 2017 targets — on institutional delivery, immunisation, under-five mortality and TFR — are tracked against NFHS and SRS. India's DHS comparability also feeds international estimates used by UN agencies.

Use in: SDG localisation, India's global rankings, health policy targets

4. It is how flagship schemes are actually evaluated

Almost every large social-sector programme has its outcome indicator inside NFHS: POSHAN Abhiyaan (stunting, wasting), Anaemia Mukt Bharat (anaemia), Janani Suraksha Yojana and PMSMA (institutional delivery, ANC), Mission Indradhanush (full immunisation), Ayushman Bharat–PMJAY (health insurance coverage), PM Jan Dhan (women's bank accounts), Ujjwala (clean fuel), Swachh Bharat (sanitation), Mission Parivar Vikas (unmet need, CPR). Administrative data reports what was delivered; NFHS reports whether it changed outcomes in the household.

Use in: scheme evaluation, outcome vs output budgeting, GS-2 social justice

5. It is the only equity lens at household level

Because NFHS collects unit-level records, outcomes can be cross-tabulated by wealth quintile, caste group, religion, mother's education, birth order and residence simultaneously. No other Indian dataset lets you ask whether a scheme reached the poorest quintile of Scheduled Tribe households in a particular district. SDG target 17.18 explicitly requires exactly this kind of disaggregation — which is why fragmenting NFHS across other systems is treated as an equity issue, not merely a statistical one.

Use in: inclusive development, social justice, data disaggregation

6. It supplies the demographic evidence for contested federal questions

NFHS fertility data sits underneath two live constitutional debates: delimitation of Lok Sabha seats after the freeze under the 84th Amendment expires, and horizontal devolution formulae before the Sixteenth Finance Commission. States that achieved fertility decline early argue they should not be penalised for demographic success. A TFR of 2.0 nationally — with southern States well below it — is therefore a federalism statistic as much as a health one.

Use in: GS-2 federalism, delimitation, Finance Commission, population policy
Evolution of the survey

What each round contributed

NFHS-1 1992–93
Established the baseline. First nationally representative estimates of fertility, infant mortality, family planning and maternal care. Designed as India's arm of the international Demographic and Health Survey programme, which is why its indicators are internationally comparable.
NFHS-2 1998–99
Introduced anaemia testing and nutritional status measurement, plus quality-of-care and domestic violence modules. State-level estimates for 26 States.
NFHS-3 2005–06
First to interview men and never-married women, and the first with HIV testing. Broadened the survey from a "mother and child" instrument into a population health instrument.
NFHS-4 2015–16
The transformative round: sample expanded to ~6 lakh households, producing district-level estimates for 640 districts for the first time. Became the baseline for the National MPI and the Aspirational Districts Programme.
NFHS-5 2019–21
Reported TFR at 2.0, below replacement for the first time, and a population sex ratio of 1,020 women per 1,000 men. Also reported worsening anaemia — 67% of children under five, up from 58.6% — which is the backdrop to the NFHS-6 controversy.
NFHS-6 2023–24
Largest coverage yet at 715 districts and 6.79 lakh households; first round fully financed by the Government of India; new modules on DBT, SHGs and digital-financial inclusion; and the first round to reduce its indicator set, from 131 to 101.

Exclusive breastfeeding: the hidden reversal

Several large States recorded steep declines in exclusive breastfeeding under six months: Haryana fell 28.3 percentage points (69.5% to 41.2%), Uttar Pradesh 25.1 points (59.7% to 34.6%), Madhya Pradesh 17.6 points (74.0% to 56.4%) and Rajasthan 16.1 points (70.4% to 54.3%). Kerala moved the other way, rising 17.2 points to 72.7%, and the Andaman & Nicobar Islands recorded the highest prevalence at 85.6%.

This is a rare NFHS-6 finding that cuts against the official narrative, which is exactly why it is worth citing. Keep it distinct from the headline that 95.6% of infants under six months were breastfed — that indicator counts any breastfeeding, not exclusive breastfeeding.

Where else the gradient shows

  • C-sections: about 40.5% in urban areas against 22.8% rural; Telangana, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu report some of the highest State rates — the same States that perform best on maternal survival.
  • Fertility: national TFR of 2.0 conceals urban fertility well below replacement and high-fertility pockets in Bihar and Uttar Pradesh, which is the crux of the delimitation debate.
  • Stunting, urban vs rural: the national gap persists; in Dadra & Nagar Haveli rural stunting runs far above the urban figure.
  • Vaccination source: 95.6% of children received most vaccinations at a public facility, and rural full-immunisation coverage runs slightly above urban — a useful counter-example to the assumption that urban always wins.

What went missing, and why it is the story

Forty-three indicators were dropped from the fact sheet and thirteen added. The omissions cut across health outcomes, vital statistics and living conditions. Hover a card to see it redacted.

The anaemia question

The most contested drop. NFHS-5 found anaemia in about 67% of children aged 6–59 months — up from 58.6% in NFHS-4 — and in roughly 57% of women aged 15–49. NFHS-6 reports no anaemia prevalence at all.

GovtHaemoglobin testing was not carried out because the finger-prick (capillary) method used earlier is known to overstate anaemia. Estimates will instead come from ICMR's Diet and Biomarkers Survey in India (DABS-I), which uses venous blood, the accepted reference standard. Officials also stress that fact sheets are the first stage of dissemination and a detailed national report with more indicators is still to come.
CriticsThe survey continues to measure the inputs — iron and folic acid supplementation during pregnancy — while dropping the outcome those inputs exist to change. That makes Anaemia Mukt Bharat unevaluable. Removing the series also breaks a trend line running back to NFHS-2 (1998–99), and the replacement survey is not designed to produce district-level, equity-disaggregated estimates on the same sample.

Where the indicators were redirected

  • Mortality (IMR, U5MR, NMR): deferred to the Sample Registration System. NFHS-5 had IMR 35, U5MR 42 and NMR 24.9 per 1,000 live births. SRS reports at State level, not district.
  • Sex ratio at birth: dropped. NFHS-5 reported 929 females per 1,000 males — a key PCPNDT Act monitoring number.
  • Sanitation and clean cooking fuel: routed to Swachh Survekshan and MoSPI surveys. Both are National MPI indicators.
  • Disability: deferred to SRS and the Census — itself overdue.
  • HIV knowledge, cancer screening, family-planning counselling quality: promised in the full national report.

The balanced line to write

Both propositions can be true. Capillary versus venous haemoglobin measurement is a genuine, documented methodological problem, and routing an indicator to the most authoritative instrument can improve estimate quality. But statistical practice requires that a series be retired only after an overlap or bridging estimate establishes comparability — you publish the new alongside the old before you discontinue the old. Discontinuing first, in the same round in which the underlying programme was due for evaluation, converts a defensible technical choice into an accountability gap. The deeper cost is structural: a single household survey that measured outcome, input and household characteristics together allowed equity analysis that four separate systems, each on a different sample and cycle, cannot reproduce.

The wider argument: statistical governance

Lift the answer beyond NFHS by placing it in the sequence of debates over India's statistical system — the withheld 2017–18 Periodic Labour Force Survey results, the questioned back-series for GDP, the postponed Census, and resignations from statistical advisory bodies. The institutional counterweights already exist and are worth naming: the National Statistical Commission (recommended by the Rangarajan Commission, constituted 2005) as an autonomous apex body, the Standing Committee on Statistics constituted in 2023, the Collection of Statistics Act 2008, and the UN Fundamental Principles of Official Statistics, which require professional independence in deciding methods and the timely, impartial release of data. The reform ask that follows naturally in a conclusion: statutory backing for the NSC, pre-announced indicator sets fixed before fieldwork, mandatory release of microdata, and an independent methodological review published alongside any decision to discontinue a series.

Eight framings that turn data into argument

Each one gives you a thesis, the evidence to support it, the schemes and institutions to name, and where to deploy it. Do not reproduce all eight in one answer — pick the two that fit the question's verb.

01 The dual burden of malnutrition +
Thesis
India is no longer fighting a hunger problem; it is fighting undernutrition and overnutrition simultaneously, often within the same household and sometimes within the same person.
Evidence
  • Stunting fell 35.5% → 29.3% and severe wasting 7.7% → 5.2%, but underweight moved only 32.1% → 31.8% and wasting only 19.3% → 19.0%.
  • Adult obesity rose sharply: women 24.0% → 30.7% (42.8% urban), men 22.9% → 27.3%. Over the longer arc, women's overweight/obesity has gone from 12.6% in 2005–06 to 30.7%.
  • High blood sugar rose for women 13.5% → 17.8% and men 15.6% → 20.9%.
  • About 40.5% of children aged 6–8 months still receive no solid or semi-solid food alongside breastmilk.
Name these
POSHAN Abhiyaan 2.0 and Saksham Anganwadi, ICDS, PM-POSHAN, Eat Right India and FSSAI front-of-pack labelling, the National Programme for Prevention and Control of NCDs, WHO's stunting target under SDG 2.2. Note the stunting–wasting divergence: stunting responds to long-run maternal and sanitation improvements, wasting to acute food and illness shocks — which is why the two indicators move differently and why a single nutrition scheme cannot fix both.
Deploy for
Nutrition policy, POSHAN evaluation, NCD burden, food-system questions, "growth without nutrition" arguments.
02 Statistical governance: when the measure disappears +
Thesis
The credibility of evidence-based policy rests on the independence and continuity of the statistical system. Dropping 43 indicators in the round that would have evaluated flagship programmes is a governance question before it is a health question.
Evidence
131 → 101 indicators; anaemia, IMR, U5MR, sex ratio at birth, sanitation and clean fuel removed; IFA supplementation (the input) retained while anaemia (the outcome) was dropped; fact sheets released in May 2026 for fieldwork completed in 2024.
Name these
National Statistical Commission and the Rangarajan Commission, Standing Committee on Statistics (2023), Collection of Statistics Act 2008, UN Fundamental Principles of Official Statistics, SDG target 17.18 on disaggregated data. Draw the parallel to the withheld PLFS 2017–18 results and the delayed Census.
Balance it
Concede the methodological case — capillary versus venous haemoglobin is a real measurement problem and ICMR's DABS is a better instrument for prevalence. Then argue the sequencing point: bridge estimates before discontinuation.
Deploy for
Governance, transparency and accountability, role of institutions, evidence-based policymaking, ethics questions on truth-telling in public administration. This is the most distinctive hook NFHS-6 offers — most candidates will only report the good numbers.
03 Over-medicalisation and the regulatory vacuum +
Thesis
India solved the access problem in childbirth and inherited a quality and regulation problem. Safe motherhood gains now coexist with avoidable surgical risk concentrated in the private sector.
Evidence
C-sections rose 21.5% → 27.2% nationally; 54.1% in private facilities against 16.9% in public; roughly 40.5% urban against 22.8% rural. WHO's population-level reference range is 10–15%. Southern States with the best maternal outcomes also report the highest C-section rates.
Name these
Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Act 2010 and its uneven State adoption, National Quality Assurance Standards and LaQshya, supplier-induced demand as a market failure under information asymmetry, PMJAY package rates as an incentive design problem, the case for mandatory Robson classification audit of caesarean indications.
Deploy for
Health-system regulation, public versus private provisioning, quality of care, market failure in social sectors, consumer protection in health.
04 Financial protection without financial security +
Thesis
Insurance coverage is not the same as protection from impoverishment. NFHS-6 measures whether a household is covered, not whether it was spared catastrophic expenditure.
Evidence
Household health insurance coverage jumped 41.0% → 60.2%, the single largest gain in the survey, credited to Ayushman Bharat–PMJAY and State schemes. Yet PMJAY covers mainly secondary and tertiary hospitalisation; outpatient care, diagnostics and medicines — which drive most out-of-pocket expenditure in India — sit outside it.
Name these
Ayushman Bharat's twin pillars (PM-JAY and Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, formerly health and wellness centres), the National Health Policy 2017 target of 2.5% of GDP in public health spending, out-of-pocket expenditure as a share of total health expenditure in the National Health Accounts, PM-ABHIM, the 15th Finance Commission's health grants through local bodies.
Deploy for
Universal health coverage, health financing, social security, the difference between coverage and outcomes.
05 Women's agency: access rose faster than autonomy +
Thesis
NFHS-6 records a genuine transformation in women's access to instruments — phones, accounts, the internet — while the indicators that would show whether access converted into decision-making power were not refreshed in the fact sheet.
Evidence
Women who have ever used the internet nearly doubled, 33.3% → 64.3%; women operating their own bank account 78.6% → 89.0% (from about 15% in 2005–06); mobile ownership 53.9% → 63.6%; hygienic menstrual protection among 15–24s 77.6% → 79.2%. New modules on DBT and self-help group membership were added.
The tension
Sex ratio at birth was dropped, and several autonomy and spousal-violence cross-tabs await the full report. Ownership of a device is not control over it; an account in a woman's name is not necessarily an account she controls. Cite Amartya Sen's distinction between resources and capabilities, or Naila Kabeer's framework of resources, agency and achievements.
Deploy for
GS-I society and gender, digital divide, financial inclusion, DBT-based welfare, women's empowerment questions in GS-II.
06 Demographic transition: below replacement, ageing early +
Thesis
India's fertility transition is complete at the national level. The policy question has shifted from controlling population growth to managing the consequences of its slowdown — and to deciding whether States that achieved it should bear a political cost.
Evidence
TFR steady at 2.0, below the replacement level of 2.1; SRS estimates place it lower still. Contraceptive prevalence 66.7% → 69.1%; unmet need for family planning down from about 13% in NFHS-4 to 8.5%. Population aged 60 and above 11.8% → 12.9%.
Name these
National Population Policy 2000, Mission Parivar Vikas, the 84th Amendment freeze on seat delimitation and the post-2026 debate, the Sixteenth Finance Commission's devolution criteria, "ageing before becoming rich", the care economy and long-term care gap, Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act 2007, Atal Vayo Abhyuday Yojana, Ayushman Bharat's extension to those aged 70 and above.
Counterpoint to include
Below-replacement fertility does not mean population decline for decades because of population momentum; the demographic dividend window remains open but is closing State by State at very different speeds.
Deploy for
Population and demography in GS-I, federalism and delimitation in GS-II, demographic dividend and labour markets in GS-III.
07 The public system won the trust battle +
Thesis
Against the assumption that Indians are abandoning public healthcare, NFHS-6 shows the public system carrying the preventive load and earning revealed trust.
Evidence
95.6% of children received most of their vaccinations at a public health facility, up from 90.7% in NFHS-4. Full immunisation rose 77.9% (NFHS-4) → 83.8% → 87.1%. Rotavirus coverage went 36.4% → 85.4% after introduction into the Universal Immunisation Programme; measles second dose 58.6% → 71.8%; hepatitis-B birth dose 67.4% → 77.6%. Rural full-immunisation coverage runs marginally above urban.
Name these
Universal Immunisation Programme, twelve completed phases of Mission Indradhanush and Intensified Mission Indradhanush, U-WIN digital immunisation registry, eVIN cold chain, ASHA and ANM frontline workers, Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, National Health Mission infrastructure in aspirational districts.
The qualifier
Roughly 12.9% of children remain partially vaccinated or unvaccinated, and public-facility strength in prevention coexists with private dominance in curative care — visible in the C-section split.
Deploy for
Public service delivery success stories, primary healthcare, last-mile delivery, questions asking you to evaluate rather than only criticise.
08 Convergence and its limits: the federal gradient +
Thesis
Inter-State gaps are narrowing on access indicators and persisting on outcome indicators, which suggests that what travels easily between States is infrastructure and what does not is administrative capacity and social determinants.
Evidence
Stunting ranges from 16.6% in Puducherry and 20.1% in Kerala to 36.8% in Meghalaya and 35.6% in Bihar. The sharpest declines came from West Bengal (33.8% → 22.4%) and Karnataka (35.4% → 26.5%), not the richest States. Gujarat at 35.3% shows high income coexisting with high stunting.
Name these
Aspirational Districts Programme and Aspirational Blocks Programme and their NFHS-derived indicators, the Kerala and Tamil Nadu models of public provisioning, the Empowered Action Group States, NITI Aayog's Health Index, and the argument that social determinants — female literacy, age at marriage, sanitation — explain more variance than health spending alone.
Deploy for
Cooperative and competitive federalism, regional disparity, targeted versus universal schemes, SDG localisation.

Get these exactly right

Precision notes

A handful of NFHS-6 figures are routinely misquoted, and several look similar enough to be swapped under time pressure. Lock the exact wording.

Total fertility rate against the replacement level

TFR = 2.0, which is below the replacement level of 2.1

Replacement-level fertility is 2.1, not 2.0, so India sits just beneath it. Do not write "at replacement level". TFR was also 2.0 in NFHS-5, so this is stability rather than a new decline, and SRS estimates put the national figure lower still.

Breastfeeding: three different indicators

95.6% breastfed under 6 months · 50.1% breastfed within one hour of birth · exclusive breastfeeding fell steeply in several large States

These are not interchangeable. The 95.6% headline counts any breastfeeding among infants under six months. Early initiation within one hour rose 41.8% → 50.1%. Exclusive breastfeeding is a separate indicator that declined sharply in Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan. Using 95.6% to claim improved exclusive breastfeeding is a factual error.

Complementary feeding, not "minimum acceptable diet"

59.5% of children aged 6–8 months receive solid or semi-solid food with breastmilk, up from 45.9%

The flip side is the sharper line: about 40.5% still do not. Minimum acceptable diet for 6–23 month olds is a different, much lower indicator; do not substitute one for the other.

High blood sugar: national against urban

Women 17.8% national (21.9% urban) · Men 20.9% national (23.9% urban)

The urban sub-figures run several points above. Quote the national number unless you explicitly mean urban. The same discipline applies to obesity: women 30.7% national against 42.8% urban.

Contraceptive prevalence labelling

69.1% is total CPR, up from 66.7%; unmet need is 8.5%

The 66.7 → 69.1 movement is overall contraceptive prevalence, not the modern-method rate. Reported reliance on modern methods appears to have slipped as traditional methods rose, which is itself a family-planning quality argument. Unmet need has fallen from roughly 13% in NFHS-4 to 8.5%.

The four maternal-care figures that get swapped

95.9% any ANC · 76.2% first-trimester ANC · 65.2% four or more ANC visits · 90.6% institutional delivery · 91.3% skilled birth attendance · 85.3% newborn postnatal care within 2 days

Anchor them apart. The gap between 95.9% receiving any antenatal care and 65.2% receiving the recommended four visits is the argument: contact with the system is near-universal, completeness of care is not.

Immunisation: use the MoHFW series

Full immunisation (12–23 months): 77.9% (NFHS-4) → 83.8% (NFHS-5) → 87.1% (NFHS-6)

A few secondary analyses circulate a parallel series with different base values depending on whether coverage is counted from vaccination cards alone or from cards plus maternal recall. Cite the MoHFW fact-sheet figures above and you will be consistent with the official release.

"Dropped from the fact sheet" is not always "not collected"

Anaemia was genuinely not measured. Most other omissions were removed from the fact sheet, with the full national report pending.

This distinction is worth a mark in a critical answer. Haemoglobin testing was suspended, so no anaemia data exists for this round at all. For several other indicators the government's position is that fact sheets are the first stage of dissemination. Write the critique precisely and it reads as informed; write it loosely and it reads as borrowed outrage.

One standing caveat: the detailed national report is still awaited. State and district fact sheets are out, so sub-national figures can be cited; deeper cross-tabulations by wealth, caste and education should be cited directionally, and it is worth saying so in the answer.
